Can vs. Should: The Two Questions Behind AI Automation

A simple way to separate what an AI agent can do safely from what is worth automating in the first place.

Most automation advice tries to compress the decision into one tidy answer. That makes the question feel simpler, but it also hides the two risks that matter most: whether the result can be trusted, and whether the work saved is greater than the work added.

The first question is about safety. Can the task be described well enough that a system knows what good looks like? Can a person catch mistakes without turning review into a second version of the task? If the answer is no, automation may still help, but it probably belongs in a draft, checklist, or suggestion role.

The second question is about cost. A task can be perfectly safe and still not deserve a workflow. If it happens once, takes five minutes, and has no meaningful reuse, the smartest automation is often restraint. This is not anti-tool sentiment. It is tool taste.

The useful answer comes from holding both questions at once. High safety with low payoff is a nice candidate for manual work. High payoff with low safety is a candidate for guardrails, review, or a smaller scope. High safety and high payoff is where automation starts to earn its keep.
