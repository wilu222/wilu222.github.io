How the skill picks a count: CDF and inverse CDF

Interactive explainer for unpolish-ai-writing. Not part of the skill install. Needs the Plotly CDN.

CDF (cumulative distribution function) = running total of the bar heights. F(m) = P(count ≤ m). At 0 it is just the first bar. At 1 it is bar 0 + bar 1. By the last bar it is 1.

Inverse CDF (also called the quantile function, or inverse-transform sampling) = read that running total backwards. You start with a number u between 0 and 1, then take the smallest m whose running total is at least u. That m is k.

The recipe’s line k = smallest m with cdf_poisson(m; λ) ≥ u is exactly the inverse CDF. The hash only supplies u. λ only shapes the bars (and therefore the stairs).

1. Poisson bars (PMF)

How often each count would show up over many rerolls. λ shapes the bars. One draw does not live on this y-axis.

2. CDF stairs (running total)

Each step rises by that bar’s height. The dashed line is u. Walk right until the stair is above the line; drop down — that is k.

3. Inverse CDF (the recipe)

Same stairs, axes swapped: start at u on the bottom, go up to the stair, read k on the left. That is the inverse CDF (quantile of u).

4. Bars tipped over (the dart ruler)

The bar heights laid end to end so they add to 1. Same inverse CDF: u falls in a segment, that segment’s label is k.
